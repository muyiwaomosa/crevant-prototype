## Part A: Purpose and principles

### 1. Purpose

**Everyone deserves a split.**

Splitpost exists so that people who create value together (creators, the members who follow and support them, and advertisers who reach them) can each share in it fairly, through relationships they choose.

### 2. How this document is used

These principles are the test we apply to product, economic and policy decisions. When a proposal would bring in more revenue but damage creator distribution or member trust, it is checked against these principles before it goes ahead, and the result is recorded.

### 3. Principles

**3.1 Connection before attention.** We put meaningful relationships ahead of maximizing time spent. Discover helps people find new value. Home strengthens the relationships people have already chosen.

**3.2 Reliable audience distribution.** Creators have access to the audiences they build. Eligible content from creators a member follows is not suppressed by algorithm. The only limits are legitimate safety and eligibility rules, and each member's own controls (such as blocking).

**3.3 Simplicity of experience.** One account, one app, a small number of clear destinations, and one Home with Clique content first. A creator is also a member; a member can become a creator.

**3.4 Fair economic participation.** The value from content-associated advertising is shared under a published model: 40% to the creator, 20% to one platform-wide fan pool, and 40% to Splitpost, of eligible net content-associated advertising revenue. General feed advertising revenue goes 100% to Splitpost. During the pilot, all of these allocations are simulated.

**3.5 Authentic contributions.** We recognize real participation, not spam, manipulation or recruitment. Rewards come only from direct contributions; there are no multi-level or recruitment commissions, and nobody pays to participate.

**3.6 Intelligence from day one.** Every important capability produces trustworthy evidence. Actual, simulated and test data are always kept distinguishable, and we do not overstate results.

**3.7 Governance by design.** Privacy, security, transparency and accountability are built in from the start, not added later.

**3.8 Prospective economic changes with advance notice.** Changes to economic rules apply going forward. They do not reduce allocations already recorded under earlier rules, except for documented corrections, fraud reversals, refunds and legally required adjustments. Significant economic or distribution changes are announced in advance: we aim for [parameter: material change notice period], with shorter notice only for urgent safety, security or legal reasons.

### 4. Commitments that follow from the principles

Under the current model, Splitpost will not:

* remove organic content to create advertising space,
* restrict followers' access to a creator's content to push the creator toward paid promotion,
* follow creators on a member's behalf,
* use sensitive interests to target ads,
* reward members with money for watching ads.

Changing any of the following requires formal review against this Constitution, a recorded decision and advance notice: engagement-based ranking of followed content, paid prioritization of organic content, changes to Clique priority, and changes to the economic split.

## Part B: Stakeholder Rights and Responsibilities

### 5. Creators

| Creators can expect | Creators are expected to |
|---|---|
| Reliable access to eligible follower feeds | Publish only content they have the right to share |
| Transparent monetization rules | Avoid fraudulent engagement and misleading claims |
| Access to their own performance information | Respect members' privacy and Splitpost policies |
| Fair discovery opportunities, regardless of follower count | Not manipulate Connection Points or referrals |
| Accurate attribution of content-associated advertising | Disclose paid or sponsored relationships |
| An explanation of material restrictions on their account | Help keep the community safe and respectful |
| Complaint and appeal routes | Follow applicable laws |

### 6. Members (consumers)

| Members can expect | Members are expected to |
|---|---|
| Control over who they follow and who is in their Clique | Participate authentically |
| Access to followed creators' eligible content | Respect creators and other members |
| Transparent Connection Points and reward records | Not make fraudulent referrals or artificial engagement |
| Control over optional commercial preferences | Not exploit Splitpost incentives |
| Protection of their personal information | Respect intellectual property |
| The ability to report harmful content and block others | Follow the Community Standards |

### 7. Advertisers

| Advertisers can expect | Advertisers are expected to |
|---|---|
| Transparent, aggregate campaign performance | Provide truthful and lawful advertising |
| Accurate commercial attribution | Avoid misleading or exploitative offers |
| Protection against artificial activity | Respect members' privacy |
| Clearly defined campaign agreements | Honour agreed campaign commitments |
| Brand safety controls | Provide accurate conversion information |
| Review of their ads before they run | Not ask for prohibited targeting |

### 8. Splitpost

| Splitpost commits to | Splitpost is entitled to |
|---|---|
| Apply these principles and publish the rules | Set and enforce reasonable policies |
| Keep simulated, actual and test data distinct | Correct errors and reverse fraudulent activity |
| Explain enforcement decisions and offer appeals | Restrict accounts that break the rules |
| Give advance notice of significant changes | Change rules going forward, with notice |
| Protect personal information | Run, pause or end the pilot |
| Measure itself against its commitments | Operate a sustainable business |

## Part C: Governance

### 9. Founder-led governance during the pilot

During the pilot, the founder makes governance decisions. This keeps decisions quick while the product is small. In return:

* decisions that affect members, creators or advertisers are recorded with their reason and effective date,
* every rule (points table, economic split, frequency limits, appeal timelines) is versioned,
* privileged administrative actions are recorded in an audit trail,
* where the same person decides and reviews (for example, appeals), we say so.

Formal stakeholder voting and decentralized governance are not part of the pilot.

### 10. How a material change is made

1. Describe the proposed change.
2. Assess the impact on creators, members, advertisers and Splitpost.
3. Check it against this Constitution.
4. Approve and version it, recording the decision, reason and effective date.
5. Communicate it: what is changing, why, who is affected, when it takes effect, how existing entitlements are treated, and where to send questions.
6. Implement it and measure whether it does what was intended.

### 11. Stakeholder feedback

* Members can send feedback in the app at any time. It is optional.
* Creators and advertisers can contact [Support contact email].
* We review feedback regularly and report back on significant themes and what we changed.
* Complaints and appeals follow the procedures in the Community Standards.

### 12. Accountability measures

We plan to track, and share in summary with participants: appeal outcomes and how often decisions are overturned, time to resolve reports and appeals, material rule changes and their notice periods, and reconciliation of simulated allocations.

### 13. Changes to this Constitution

The Constitution changes through the process in section 10, with the longest notice period we give for any change.
